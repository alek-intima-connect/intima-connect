Conscientious Objector — Know Your Rights

Under 10 U.S.C. § 1552 and DoD Directive 1300.6, any service member who develops sincere beliefs — religious or deeply held moral convictions — against participation in war may apply for Conscientious Objector status. This is a legal right. The burden of proof lies on the applicant to demonstrate sincerity. Below is the framework.

01
Two Classifications

1-A-O: Will serve in a non-combatant capacity (medic, logistics). 1-O: Full discharge — opposed to all military service. Both require sincere belief. Center on Conscience & War →

02
Application Process

File DD Form 4711 (Army) or branch equivalent. Beliefs need not be religious — moral or ethical grounds qualify since United States v. Seeger (1965). Get legal counsel before filing. GI Rights Hotline →

03
You Cannot Be Punished for Applying

Retaliation against a CO applicant is illegal. However, document everything. Keep copies of all filings. Connect with an attorney before speaking to your chain of command. NLG Military Law Task Force →

04
AWOL vs. CO Status

Going AWOL is not the same as filing for CO status. AWOL carries criminal penalties. Filing for CO, regardless of outcome, is a protected legal process. Know the difference before you act. Get Counseling →
